How costs are calculated

The round-trip figure is the average cost, in dollars, of buying the quantity you entered and selling it straight back. It adds the book you cross, the broker’s own charges, and a tax when that schedule names one. The fees column is those charges without the book. N/A means the book was not measured.

The formula

On a US listing the book starts from Rule 605: the effective spread a retail order of 100 to 499 shares paid, price improvement included, in dollars per share. Nasdaq, NYSE, NYSE Arca, NYSE American and Cboe BZX share that figure. Rule 606 then scales it by where that broker-dealer sends held market and marketable-limit orders.

Q = Σt wt × meann Et,nEn
book = E × Q
En
The blended Rule 605 effective spread of name n, dollars per share, already a round trip.
Et,n
The same name, from the firm that venue t is. The mean is equal-weight over every name where both exist.
wt
The share of this broker’s held market and marketable-limit orders sent to t, from its Rule 606 report.

An exchange, an ATS or an unnamed venue is not given a ratio of 1. Its ratio is the reconstructed quoted spread divided by the blended effective spread. A quoted touch is wider than what retail orders actually paid, so a broker that sends most of its flow there has a Q above 1.

Outside the US the book is a two-sided bid and ask, in basis points. Each reading is brought back to the average hour of the session, and the published figure is the median of those readings. Q is not applied.

bp = ask − bidmid × 10,000
book = amount × bp / 10,000

The round trip the page prints is that book, plus the broker’s charges, plus a named tax.
